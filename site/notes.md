## Septic system (owner, Oct 11 2026)

- **Conventional gravity system:** a 1,200-gallon concrete tank and a standard drainfield
  (buried trenches). No pump, panel or spray heads.
- **Tank:** typically ~5 × 10 ft with two concrete lids (inlet and outlet) ~6–8 ft apart,
  often buried 6–24 in. It's usually 5–20 ft out from where the main drain leaves the
  house, which is the sewer cleanout (worksheet 13c).
- **Drainfield:** trenches leave the tank's outlet end and run downhill (gravity).
  Inspection ports are white capped pipes at trench ends. Look for green stripes in dry
  weather.
- **Rules of thumb (confirm with the county):**
  - Nothing built on the tank or the field.
  - No driving or parking on the field.
  - Keep ~5 ft between structures and the tank or field.
  - Keep the lids reachable, and the pump truck within hose reach of them.

## Decision: site B (Oct 11 2026)

The owner wants the building at **site B, the old carport spot**, so that it also **shields
the house and yard from US-290 traffic noise**. Working footprint: **20 × 40 ft, square to
the house** (24 × 40 if the trees at its SW corner allow).

### Making B a good sound barrier

- **Solid highway side.** No doors, windows or vents on the north (US-290) wall. Put the
  roll-up doors on the south or east side, away from the highway.
- **Height helps.** The building only shields what it hides from the traffic. A taller
  eave (~12–14 ft) covers more of the yard and the house's lower floor; second-story
  windows will partly see over it.
- **Mass and insulation.** Bare sheet metal is light and rings. Insulated wall panels, or
  insulation plus an interior liner, cut both sound through the wall and rain noise inside.
- **No gaps.** Sound leaks around the ends and under the walls. Keep the slab edge tight to
  grade, and tie the building's ends visually and physically into the existing tall fence.
- **Longer is better.** It's 40 ft now, limited by the oak (east) and the septic pipe's
  10 ft keep-out (west). If probing shows the pipe is only an inspection port and nothing
  runs toward B, the building may stretch a few feet west.
- **Expectation:** behind a solid building that blocks the line of sight, traffic noise
  typically drops ~5–10 dB, noticeably quieter. The effect is strongest in its "shadow"
  close behind it.

### Checks that matter most for B

- **The low wire (shot 20):** where it runs from the pole beyond the fence to the house. If
  it crosses B, the utility will need to raise, reroute or underground it before the steel
  goes up.
- **Probe B for septic** (a trench from the pipe toward B?), and between the pipe and B.
- **Branches over B's east end (17a)**, for erection clearance and eave height.
- **Tree edge at B's SW corner (T11)**, which decides 20 × 40 vs 24 × 40.

### What "large metal building on a slab" adds

- **Slab outside the oak's drip line**, not just its 12 ft no-dig core. A slab and its
  thickened-edge trench cut roots and seal the soil.
- **Septic:**
  - No slab over the tank or the drainfield.
  - Keep a separation from both; plan on ~10 ft until Travis County gives the number.
  - Pipe roof runoff (gutters and downspouts) away from the drainfield so it doesn't
    flood it.
- **Construction access:**
  - A loaded concrete mixer (~30 tons) can't cross the drainfield and shouldn't drive under
    the oak. Plan on a **concrete pump parked on the driveway**, with a line pump or boom
    reaching the slab.
  - The steel delivery and lift need overhead clearance (the low wire, shot 20, and the
    branches).
- **Property line:** metal walls within ~5 ft of a property line usually need fire rating
  and no openings. Plan on **5–10 ft from the surveyed line**, not the fence.
- **Paperwork:**
  - The building supplier's stamped drawings for the local wind speed, and a slab
    designed for the column and anchor-bolt loads.
  - Check Travis County's development permit, and whether its septic (OSSF) office reviews
    new structures on septic lots.
- **Fit check:**
  - West of the oak's drip line to the back-line easement is ~120 ft long. But only
    ~25–30 ft is open between a 5–10 ft line setback and the tree line, before the
    drainfield and the septic truck lane take their share.
  - A wide building may not fit there. The open back lawn west/south-west of the house
    is the other candidate: bigger, but harder to reach.
